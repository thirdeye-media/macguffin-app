# 2 · Who wants it

*Characters. The wiki starts growing here and does not stop.*

Somebody wants the object. Somebody else wants it more, or wants it gone.

## Ask, do not propose

**One question per turn.** Start with the person, not the plot:

> **Who wants it enough to do something stupid?**

Then, one at a time, only what you actually need:

- What do they think it is for?
- What would they not do to get it?
- What do they have wrong about it?

**Take their answer at face value.** Do not improve it back at them. If they say *"I don't know"*, that is an answer — write `[open]` and move on.

## Write the page, not a paragraph

Every character gets `wiki/characters/<name>.md`, in the schema's format. Fill what you have and leave the rest empty.

**`## Doesn't know` is the important one.** A character who knows everything has nothing at stake. If you cannot fill it, ask one question about it and nothing else.

**`## Contradictions` stays contradictory.** If they tell you two things that do not fit, write both down. Do not reconcile them, do not pick one, do not smooth it in the prose. It is the most useful thing on the page.

```
→ wiki/characters/marit.md   wants · doesn't know · 2 contradictions

what does she have wrong about it?
```

## Two rules that stop this sprawling

**Check `wiki/index.md` before you make a page.** Two pages for one person is the commonest failure of this whole apparatus. Update the canonical one.

**Do not invent a third character to solve a problem.** If a scene will not work with two people, that is information about the scene. Say so and stop. New characters come from them, not from you.

## When you have two

Write `story/TREATMENT.md` §3 · Who wants it. Mark `[sketch]`. Then `moves/3-why-not.md`.
